# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Argue with the work, not the person.

## What is expected

- Assume the other person is competent and acting in good faith until shown otherwise.
- Criticise code, claims, benchmarks and designs freely and directly — that is the point of the
  project being public.
- Accept correction the same way you give it. A benchmark that turns out to be unfair gets
  republished, not defended.
- Respect that people contribute on their own time, in their own languages, from their own
  timezones.

## What is not

- Personal attacks, insults, or derision — including toward maintainers of other projects. Rift
  exists because Mountebank designed a good API, and Mountebank, WireMock and Microcks each do
  things better than Rift does. Comparisons here are about architecture, never about quality of
  character or competence.
- Harassment of any kind, public or private. Sexualised language or imagery. Unwelcome attention.
- Discrimination or demeaning comments on the basis of gender, gender identity or expression,
  sexual orientation, disability, physical appearance, body size, race, ethnicity, age, religion,
  or nationality.
- Publishing others' private information without explicit permission.

## Scope

This applies in the issue tracker, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, and anywhere someone
is representing the project.

## Enforcement

Report to **zainalpour@gmail.com**. Reports are handled privately and the reporter's identity is not
disclosed without their consent.

The maintainer may edit or delete comments, close issues or PRs, and block accounts. For a first
incident the usual response is a private request to stop; repeated or severe behaviour results in a
ban without further warning.

I am one person, not a committee, and I will not always get this right. If you think a decision was
wrong, say so — by email if that is easier than in public.
